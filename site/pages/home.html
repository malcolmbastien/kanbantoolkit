For the whole team

The cards hold the detail. The initiatives show how it adds up.

A team board does both at once. Its cards flows are a kanban the team shapes for its own work — columns, splits and WIP limits, and a second flow when another kind of work needs its own lane. The initiatives lane above reads those same cards as the summary that leaders and stakeholders see, so nothing is entered twice.

The cards flow

A kanban the team shapes

Columns and splits follow the steps the team actually uses, and a WIP limit caps what is in flight. A second flow gives a different kind of work — a support queue, say — its own columns and its own limit.

  • One or more cards flows, each with its own columns
  • Split a column into the steps the team uses
  • A WIP limit per column, at or over the line
  • Every card reachable without the mouse

The initiatives lane

The summary that leaders and stakeholders read

Above the cards, the initiatives lane groups the outcomes the team delivers and counts progress, dates and health from the cards beneath it — the same work, not a second report.

  • Progress and health rolled up as the cards move
  • One summary to share with leaders and stakeholders
  • The working detail stays with the team, not in the summary
  • Both views read one board and one set of cards

Warnings and roll-ups, counted from the work. A card says when its planned start has slipped, when it is overdue or stale, when it is blocked, or when it is waiting on a dependency. The same rules read the hierarchy, so an initiative reports what needs attention below it. No flag is set by hand, and each clears when the work moves.

  • late startThe planned start has passed, but the work has not begun.
  • 3d overdueThe promised day has passed, and the work is not done.
  • still 17dCommitted work that has not moved for a week.
  • blockedThe reason is written on the card, not hidden in a chat.
  • 2 openWork it depends on, whether here or on another board.

Every level of work

Team, program and portfolio boards, in one workspace.

A board's type decides what it shows. Team boards hold the cards; program boards coordinate the teams beneath them; portfolio boards read the work against the strategy it serves. Each level is a view of the same work, not another copy of it.

Team

The day-to-day work

The team's own cards flows, columns and WIP limits, together with the initiatives its work rolls up into. This is where cards are created and moved.

  • Cards, cards flows and the initiatives lane
  • Dashboard, timeline and flow metrics
  • The work each person holds now

Program

How teams deliver together

A coordination board over the teams beneath it: its own initiatives, plus a band for each linked board, read for progress, dependencies, and where one team is waiting on another.

  • One band per linked team or program
  • Progress and risk rolled up, read-only
  • Dependencies across team boundaries

Portfolio

How the work serves strategy

A strategy map of objectives, each holding the initiatives that serve it — from any board — with coverage gaps stated in words and progress counted from the work.

  • Objectives and the initiatives mapped to them
  • Coverage: objectives with no initiative, and initiatives serving no objective
  • Progress and health for each objective

Private by default

Your board stays on your own server

Nothing leaves the machine unless you turn on webhooks or mail.

Your work stays yours

Your board is not a tenant inside somebody else's service. It is a database file on a machine you control, with no vendor account in the path — copy it, move it or restore it whenever you want.

No ads or analytics

No third-party scripts, no tracking, and nothing used to train anything. Email, when you turn it on, goes through the relay you configure — not a mailing service.
