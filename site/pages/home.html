Three ideas, and what they mean for you

Work is a graph. Cards, initiatives, objectives and the edges between them are items in one model, not folders in a tree. The consequence: the same card can be read on the team's board and on the programme's without being copied, so it cannot disagree with itself.

Facts are derived. A rollup, a status, a dependency that has cleared, a stale initiative — each is computed from the work, never entered. The consequence: nobody's Tuesday is spent updating a status that the cards already answer, and the number cannot drift from the work.

One truth, two audiences. The team shapes its own columns and names them what it likes; management reads the same work at a higher altitude. The consequence: a board can be reorganised without the portfolio becoming wrong, because the portfolio is not a copy of the board.

Who it is for

A programme whose board no longer matches the work. The columns drifted, the status is a week old, and the slide is a third thing again.

A team whose column names are its own. Not somebody else's idea of a pipeline, and not one that has to be renamed to fit a report.

Somebody who reads for a living, and is tired of asking. The numbers are there because the work is; nobody has to be chased for them.

Four questions

Do I need an account?
Not to look. The demo is the shipped board with seeded work and no session. An account belongs to an instance you run yourself.
What happens offline?
An edit you make with no connection is held, in order, and sent when the line comes back. The app says how many are waiting; nothing is silently dropped.
How do we run it ourselves?
Clone it, build it, run it: three commands. The same routes and rules run on a Node process and on a Cloudflare Worker, and both entries are in the repository.
Do you train anything on our data?
No. Your work is not used to train anything, and there is no third party in the path to read it — the privacy notice lists every service that touches it.
