Features

The board is the source of truth. Every picture below is of the shipped build, and every view reads the same cards — no second status report, and no copy that can drift.

One card, wherever it is read

A card is an item; where you read it is a placement — one per board. Moving it moves it, so the same card on a team board and a programme board is one thing, not two that slowly disagree. Its type, its parent, its assignees and its history come with it.

A card can also point elsewhere: a ticket, a design, a contract. The link is a label and an address the app never fetches, so it stays a way out to where the work lives rather than another integration.

Flows shaped around your team

Every top-level column is one of four stages — Backlog, Not Started, In Progress, Done — and that is fixed. The shape between them is yours: split a column into the cells the team actually uses, name them, and put a WIP limit on the one that needs it. A limit can tint the lane, ask for a reason before a move, or hold the move.

A board carries one initiatives flow and one or more cards flows — a delivery flow and a support queue, each with its own columns and its own limit. They sit as bands rather than tabs, so a move between flows is a move you can watch.

Three levels of detail, one board

The Detail dial chooses how much of a card the board draws, per board and remembered in this browser. Full is every field. Summary keeps the title, type, people, due date and dependency count, with no warning badges: warnings are Full's to spell out and Mini's to mark. Mini is a colour tile that opens a reading card on hover or focus. It is a reading choice, not a permission, and design mode always shows Full.

See the connections, not just the cards

The Connections lens draws the graph the board already holds: solid blue strings from a parent to its children, solid red strings from a waiting item to the work it waits on, with a circle at the start and an arrow at the end. An external dependency runs from the card here to the External dependencies card below. It is off by default, remembered per board, and takes no click — a reading aid, not a mode.

A block says why, and clears itself

A block is a statement with a reason in the team's own words, shown on the card and in its panel. A dependency is an edge, and its state is read from the two ends: open while the work it waits on is open, cleared when that work is done. There is no field to remember to update.

When the other end lives on another board, the External dependencies band below the cards places it in the stage its own rollup gives it, names its home board, and lists the cards here that wait on it. The dependency is never moved or copied onto this board.

Initiatives, computed from the work below

An initiative has no status field and nothing to estimate on its own. Its status is the rollup of its leaves, and its completion is the share of them that are done — one issue, one share, counted rather than weighted. A Backlog leaf counts in the percentage without dragging a committed initiative backwards, and when committed work sits over unplanned leaves the card warns rather than correcting itself.

Objectives over the work

A portfolio board leads with a strategy map. Each objective heads the board's own initiatives flow, narrowed to the initiatives attributed to it — from any board — and the two coverage gaps are said where they occur: an objective with no initiative behind it, and an initiative that serves no objective.

Progress comes from the leaves below, and a period says when it is due. Key results are statements, not typed scores, so an objective cannot claim progress the work does not show.

Flow, measured from the moves

Every move is already in the card's history, so lead and cycle time, their distributions, throughput and a forecast are read from the log rather than compiled by hand. The Flow view shows the delivery scatter and its weekly range, the cycle-time distribution, aging work, WIP by age, net flow and a service level you can read from either end — choose a confidence for the days, or a duration for the share. Range, flow and card-type filters make it one team's reading, and the address carries it. Done means the card reached Done.

One model, every altitude

A team board is one view. Program and portfolio boards aggregate the boards beneath them; the dashboard surfaces what needs attention; the timeline places initiatives against their target dates, names each row's window, and points to bars outside the visible slice; the Flow view measures the same cards. The altitudes are lenses over one model, not copies of it: the numbers at the top are the numbers at the bottom.

What comes back to you

Watch a card or an initiative and the inbox collects what happens next: a move, a block, an edit, a comment, a due-date change, an initiative's health. A watch starts when you make it, so the card's earlier record stays in its History rather than arriving as a feed of old news. The creator, each assignee and a comment's author watch without asking; a mention does not subscribe anybody.

If the instance has a mail relay, a person can also turn on email in their profile — immediate, daily or weekly, at 08:00 in their own time zone. One plain-text message per period is written at send time from the same outbox the inbox reads: watched changes, assignments, mentions, due work and watched initiatives at risk. It carries a tokenized unsubscribe link and a preferences link, and it has no remote image or tracking pixel. With no relay, the feature is absent rather than broken and the profile says why.

What is deliberately not automated

The app writes nothing nobody wrote. No generated cards, no summarised history, no AI guesses about what a card means. A model that fills a board with plausible sentences makes the board a second author of the team's work, and then nobody can tell which parts are true.
