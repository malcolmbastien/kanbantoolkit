How it works

The model in five ideas and one refusal, each with a picture of the shipped build.

One card, many boards

A card is an item, and where it is read is a placement: one per board. Move it, and it moves — not a copy that can drift. It keeps its type, its parent, its assignees and its history wherever it appears.

A card can also point elsewhere: a ticket, a design, a contract. The link is a reference — a label and an address the app never fetches — so it stays a way out to where the work lives, not a second integration.

The lane tree, and the four stages

A board is a set of flows, each a tree of lanes, and every top-level column is one of four stages: Backlog, Not Started, In Progress, Done. Split a column into cells and name them what your team calls them — the stage stays the column's, so a card's status is never a word somebody chose on a Tuesday. The four are fixed; the shape between them is yours.

A board carries one initiatives flow and one or more cards flows — a delivery flow and a support queue, each with its own columns, its own name and its own WIP limit. They are stacked as bands rather than hidden behind tabs, so a card dragged from one to the other is a move between two visible trees.

Initiatives, computed from the work below

An initiative has no status field and nothing to estimate. Its status is the rollup of its leaves, and its completion is the share of them that are done — one issue, one share, counted rather than weighted. Add an unplanned card and the number moves back.

Dependencies that clear themselves

A dependency is an edge between two items. Its state is read from the two ends, so it is blocked only while the thing it waits on is open: there is no field to remember to update.

The same graph at three altitudes

A team board is one view. A program board aggregates the teams under it, and a portfolio reads the objectives the work serves. The altitudes are lenses over one model, not copies of it: the numbers at the top are the numbers at the bottom.

What comes back to you

Watch a card or an initiative and the inbox collects what happens next: a move, a block, an edit, a note, a due-date change, an initiative's health. A watch starts when you make it, so the card's earlier history stays in its Activity rather than arriving as a feed of old news. The creator, each assignee and a note's author watch without asking; a mention does not subscribe anybody.

If the instance has a mail relay, a person can also turn on email in their profile — immediate, daily or weekly, at 08:00 in their own time zone. One plain-text message per period is written at send time from the same outbox the inbox reads: watched changes, assignments, mentions, due work and watched initiatives at risk. It carries a tokenized unsubscribe link and a preferences link, and it has no remote image or tracking pixel. With no relay, the feature is absent rather than broken and the profile says why.

What is deliberately not automated

The app writes nothing nobody wrote. No generated cards, no summarised history, no AI guesses about what a card means. A model that fills a board with plausible sentences makes the board a second author of the team's work, and then nobody can tell which parts are true.
